The shell prints in one piece standing on its back, the clear front is a hexagon of acrylic or PET in a magnetic frame, and a small wedge at the front holds the substrate.

Two straight walls meet at a right angle at the back, so it stands snugly in the corner of a shelf or room; the front is a quarter circle. Side length is measured along each straight wall.

A glass-house dome: a straight clear wall stands in the base, and on it a ring carries printed ribs that rise to a mesh cap. Flat PET panels spring into slots in the ribs, so nothing clear is printed. Lift the cap off to feed; a 9 cm lamp sits on the cap.

A Victorian birdcage: a round printed cage with fine bars and a bell-shaped top, printed standing up in one piece, on a base with substrate. Lift the cage off the base to feed or clean; the mantis stays on the bars.

A nursery cabinet for nymphs: every drawer has its own cubby. Print one cubby and one drawer per nymph and join the cubbies with the little bow-tie keys, as wide and tall as you like. Each drawer has a mesh front window, a feeding port with a foam plug and a clip-on label.

The wall is either a clear PET sheet (0.5–1 mm, sold as "PET-Folie" or "PETG-Platte") rolled into grooves in the base and lid, or a printed mesh tube for full ventilation. You open it by lifting the lid, or through a feeding hatch.

Floor pegs stand on the base under the substrate, 40 mm apart. Pots, dishes and twig holders made with a floor socket push onto them, so they stay put when the mantis climbs on them.

Walls and door

The swing door turns on a rod printed along its edge, which snaps into three printed clips on the frame. No pin, screws or glue. It closes onto a raised rim, held by two round magnets, a printed rib that clicks into a groove in the door, or a turn latch on the corner post that you turn over the door's edge.

In a recess, the acrylic sits flush in the front of the door: tuck its hinge-side edge under the sloped lip, press it in and turn the two buttons over the other edge. The pane stiffens the door, and you can swap it if it gets scratched.

Swappable tile panels are a frame with square openings. Each opening takes a printed mesh tile or a clear acrylic tile, held by small printed turn buttons, so you can change which parts of the wall are clear or ventilated at any time.

Click a wall in the picture to switch it between clear acrylic, printed mesh and swappable tiles. In a tile panel, click a single tile to switch it between mesh and clear.

Pin rails are rows of square holes in printed mesh panels, every 40 mm up the wall and 20 mm apart. Pieces with a pin tab lie flat against the wall and are held by two printed snap pins pushed through into the panel. Both faces of the panel stay flat.

The lid is always a printed mesh, so the mantis has a grippy ceiling to hang from when it moults. Clear panels are acrylic sheet cut to the sizes on the right. Mesh sides add cross-ventilation, which mantises from warm, humid places need.

Lid and lights

Rings and spokes cut the vents as circles round the lamp, with the lid's corners left solid. Air slots in the base let fresh air in low at the front; it rises and leaves through the lid, which keeps the clear panels from fogging. Keep the substrate below the slots.

The hatch sits where you put it. If something is in the way, such as a light window, it moves to the nearest free spot. A magnet hatch lifts off; a plug hatch pushes into the opening; a twist-lock hatch pushes in and locks with a short turn, so a mantis can't push it open. Plug and twist-lock hatches need no magnets.

Printer and filament

Set to a Creality K2 Plus (350 × 350 × 350 mm). Change it if you print on another machine.

Before you print

  • Print the base and lid flat, the posts lying down, and mesh panels flat. Each file is already turned that way.
  • Nothing needs glue: the posts sit in their sockets, the panels slide into the grooves and the lid holds it all together. Choose printed screws under Lid fixing if you want it to stay locked.
  • If you feed fruit flies to small nymphs, they can get through printed mesh. Lay fine fabric mesh under the lid for that stage; the lid's own rim holds it.
  • Wash printed parts and let them air out for a day before the mantis moves in.
